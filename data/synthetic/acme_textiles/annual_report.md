## Chairman's message and business overview

Acme Textiles Limited is a vertically integrated manufacturer of cotton yarn, knitted fabric and ready-made garments. The company operates three spinning units, one knitting and processing unit and two garment factories in Tamil Nadu, with an installed capacity of 3.6 lakh spindles and 1,100 knitting machines. It employed 6,200 people at 31 March 2025.

Exports contributed 34% of revenue in FY25 (FY24: 31%), mainly to the United Kingdom, Germany and the United States. The top five customers together accounted for 41% of revenue in FY25. Our largest single customer, a European fast-fashion retailer, accounted for 14% of revenue.

The year saw stable cotton prices in the first half and a sharp rise in the last quarter. Garment demand from export markets recovered, helped by supply-chain diversification away from other Asian origins. Domestic demand remained steady.

## Management discussion and analysis: financial performance

Revenue from operations rose to Rs 931.2 crore in FY25 from Rs 845.5 crore in FY24, a growth of 10.1%. In FY23 revenue was Rs 742.0 crore; FY24 growth was 13.9%.

EBITDA was Rs 130.4 crore in FY25 (margin 14.0%), compared with Rs 120.9 crore in FY24 (margin 14.3%) and Rs 104.9 crore in FY23 (margin 14.1%). The margin dipped by 30 basis points in FY25 because of higher cotton and freight costs in the fourth quarter.

Depreciation was Rs 46.0 crore (FY24: Rs 41.5 crore) and finance costs were Rs 38.6 crore (FY24: Rs 34.9 crore; FY23: Rs 31.4 crore). Other income was Rs 5.6 crore. Profit before tax was Rs 51.4 crore and profit after tax was Rs 38.5 crore (FY24: Rs 37.1 crore; FY23: Rs 29.5 crore). Earnings per share were Rs 16.04 on 2.4 crore shares of Rs 10 each.

## Borrowings, liquidity and capital expenditure

Total borrowings at 31 March 2025 were Rs 382.0 crore (31 March 2024: Rs 351.0 crore; 31 March 2023: Rs 330.5 crore). Of this, long-term borrowings were Rs 182.0 crore, current maturities of long-term debt Rs 32.0 crore and short-term working-capital borrowings Rs 168.0 crore. Cash and bank balances were Rs 21.4 crore and liquid investments Rs 8.6 crore, giving net debt of Rs 352.0 crore, or 2.7 times EBITDA.

Net worth was Rs 300.0 crore at year end (Rs 263.9 crore a year earlier), so total debt to equity was 1.27 times. Interest coverage (EBITDA to finance costs) was 3.4 times and the debt service coverage ratio was 1.78 times. The current ratio was 1.27 times, with current assets of Rs 413.0 crore against current liabilities of Rs 324.0 crore.

Cash flow from operations was Rs 70.4 crore after a working-capital build-up of Rs 48.0 crore, driven by higher inventory and receivables. Capital expenditure was Rs 62.0 crore, mainly modernisation of two spinning units.

The board approved a capital expenditure programme of Rs 120 crore over FY26 and FY27 to add 60,000 spindles at a new spinning unit. It will be financed by a term loan of Rs 78 crore and internal accruals of Rs 42 crore. The company expects net debt to EBITDA to peak at about 3.0 times during FY27.

Promoters held 58.4% of the equity at 31 March 2025, of which 12.0% of their holding was pledged with lenders against group borrowings.

## Auditor's report and contingent liabilities

The statutory auditors, M/s Raghavan & Iyer, Chartered Accountants, issued an unmodified opinion on the financial statements for FY25. The report includes an Emphasis of Matter paragraph drawing attention to a Goods and Services Tax demand of Rs 14.2 crore under dispute before the appellate authority. The company has not provided for this amount, as management believes it will succeed on appeal. The auditors' opinion is not modified in respect of this matter.

Contingent liabilities not provided for totalled Rs 21.6 crore at 31 March 2025, comprising the GST demand of Rs 14.2 crore and Rs 7.4 crore of bank guarantees and disputed customs duty. There were no cases of default in repayment of loans or interest to any lender during the year. No fraud was reported by the auditors under section 143(12) of the Companies Act.

## Risks

Key risks are cotton price volatility, concentration of customers, working-capital intensity (inventory and receivable days increased by 9 days in FY25), foreign-exchange exposure on export receivables (hedged up to 70% of net exposure), and execution risk on the spinning expansion.
