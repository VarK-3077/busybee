# Demo Bank of India — Corporate and SME Credit Policy (Working Capital and Term Credit)

Version 1.0. Applies to fund-based and non-fund-based limits sanctioned to non-financial
corporate and SME borrowers with aggregate bank exposure of INR 5 crore and above. This is a
demonstration policy modelled on the structure of Indian public- and private-sector bank
policies and on RBI prudential norms (IRAC norms, Large Exposure Framework, Master Direction
on Credit Facilities). It is not a real bank's policy.

All amounts are in INR crore unless stated. Ratios use the latest audited financial year
unless stated.

## 1. Purpose

The policy sets the minimum standard for accepting a corporate borrower, the financial and
non-financial thresholds that a credit proposal must meet, the red flags that force a
referral or decline, the method for sizing a working-capital limit, and the three bank-defined
scores that every credit review must report: the Financial Strength Score, the Internal Risk
Grade and the Maximum Exposure test.

## 2. Eligibility

A borrower is eligible for consideration only if all of the following hold:

1. It is a company, LLP or partnership registered in India with at least 3 years of audited
   operating history (2 years if promoted by a group with a rating of A- or better).
2. Its net worth (tangible net worth, TNW) is positive.
3. It has no account classified as Special Mention Account SMA-2 or worse, and no account
   classified as NPA, with any lender in the last 36 months.
4. It is not on the RBI wilful-defaulter list, the CIBIL suit-filed list, or the bank's
   negative list (section 8).
5. Its main business is not in the excluded sectors: speculative real estate, trading in
   commodities on margin, gambling, and activities on the bank's environmental exclusion list.

## 3. Documents required

* Audited financial statements for the last 3 years, with auditor's report and notes.
* Annual report for the latest year, including the board's report and the Management
  Discussion and Analysis.
* Provisional financials for the current year (not older than 6 months) and projections for
  the next 2 years including projected turnover.
* A valid external credit rating rationale from a SEBI-registered agency (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE,
  India Ratings, Acuite, Brickwork), not older than 12 months.
* Bank statements of all operating accounts for the last 12 months and a statement of all
  existing borrowings, repayment schedule and charges created.
* Shareholding pattern with details of promoter pledge, and a list of directors.
* Details of pending litigation and regulatory proceedings above INR 1 crore.
* Latest GST returns and income-tax returns for 3 years.

A proposal missing the audited statements or the external rating cannot be scored.

## 4. Financial ratio thresholds

Definitions: EBITDA is operating profit before depreciation, interest and tax, excluding
other income. Net debt is total borrowings (short and long term, including current maturities
and lease liabilities) less cash and bank balances and liquid investments. Interest is the
gross finance cost. CFADS is cash flow available for debt service, taken as EBITDA less cash
taxes paid. Debt service is interest paid plus scheduled principal repayments in the year.
Total debt is total borrowings.

Components are read as follows. Total borrowings are short-term plus long-term borrowings,
including current maturities. Lease liabilities are current plus non-current. Liquid
investments are current investments only (mutual funds, treasury bills and fixed deposits under
current assets), never non-current investments or investments in subsidiaries, associates or
joint ventures. Cash taxes are income tax paid, net of refunds, from the cash-flow statement,
not the tax expense. A figure that cannot be found in this form is reported as missing, and a
ratio computed without it, or with a stand-in for it, is a caution and never a plain pass.

Basis: all ratios use consolidated figures when consolidated financial statements exist, and
standalone figures otherwise, with a note saying so. Consolidated and standalone figures are
never mixed within one ratio.

| Ratio | Minimum standard (must meet) | Caution zone (refer to credit committee) |
|---|---|---|
| Net debt / EBITDA | at most 4.5 times | above 3.5 and up to 4.5 times |
| Interest coverage (EBITDA / interest) | at least 2.5 times | below 4.0 and at least 2.5 times |
| DSCR (CFADS / debt service) | at least 1.25 times | below 1.50 and at least 1.25 times |
| Current ratio | at least 1.10 times | below 1.33 and at least 1.10 times |
| Total debt / equity | at most 3.0 times | above 2.0 and up to 3.0 times |
| Revenue trend (year on year) | not below -10 percent | below 0 and at least -10 percent |

If EBITDA is zero or negative, net debt / EBITDA and interest coverage fail automatically.
If debt service is zero, DSCR is treated as meeting every threshold. Net debt / EBITDA is
tested as 2 x net debt <= 9 x EBITDA at the 4.5 times line, and other ratios likewise by
cross-multiplication.

## 5. External rating, promoter holding and pledge

* Minimum external rating: BBB- (long-term) or A3 (short-term) from a SEBI-registered agency.
  Ratings below BBB- are not acceptable except for secured SME limits below INR 25 crore.
* Rating outlook negative or rating under watch with negative implications moves the
  proposal to the caution zone.
* Promoter holding (including promoter group) must be at least 26 percent for listed
  companies, and promoters must have a continuing management role.
* Promoter shares pledged: up to 25 percent of promoter holding is acceptable; above 25
  and up to 50 percent is caution; above 50 percent is a major red flag.

For rating comparisons the bank uses a numeric index, lower being better:
AAA = 1, AA+ = 2, AA = 3, AA- = 4, A+ = 5, A = 6, A- = 7, BBB+ = 8, BBB = 9, BBB- = 10,
BB+ = 11, BB = 12, BB- = 13, B+ = 14, B = 15, B- = 16, C = 17 and below, D = 22 (default).

## 6. Red flags

Each of the following is a **major red flag**; two or more is a mandatory referral to the
credit committee and one raises the Internal Risk Grade by one notch (section 9):

1. Qualified or adverse audit opinion, disclaimer of opinion, or emphasis of matter on going
   concern.
2. Any delay of more than 30 days in debt servicing in the last 24 months (SMA-1 or worse).
3. Promoter pledge above 50 percent of promoter holding.
4. Material litigation or regulatory proceedings (claim or penalty above 10 percent of net
   worth) that is not provided for.
5. Auditor resignation before the end of the term without reasons, or change of statutory
   auditor twice in 3 years.
6. Related-party transactions with advances or loans to promoter entities above 10 percent
   of net worth.
7. Material negative adverse media regarding fraud, forensic audit or investigation by an
   enforcement agency.

A **default flag** is raised if there is any NPA classification (90 days overdue or more
under the IRAC norms), wilful default, or one-time settlement with any lender in the last
36 months. A default flag forces the Internal Risk Grade to 8 and the decision to decline.

## 7. Working-capital limit sizing (turnover method)

For working-capital limits the bank applies the turnover method (Nayak Committee style):

* Projected annual turnover (next financial year) is the base.
* Working-capital requirement is 25 percent of projected turnover.
* The borrower must bring 5 percent of projected turnover as its own margin (net working
  capital); the bank may finance at most 20 percent of projected turnover.
* Bank finance cannot exceed 20 percent of projected turnover, and, because the limit must
  be fully drawn only against genuine sales, the projected turnover may not exceed 125
  percent of the latest audited turnover without a credit-committee exception.

## 8. Negative and caution list

Negative list (automatic decline): entities or promoters on the RBI wilful defaulter list,
SEBI debarred entities, entities with active IBC proceedings, entities flagged as fraud by
any bank under RBI fraud reporting norms, and entities under UN or OFAC sanctions.

Caution list (refer to credit committee): sectors flagged by the bank for stress at the time
of review, borrowers with customer concentration above 50 percent in one customer,
borrowers with a single-supplier dependency, and borrowers whose rating has been downgraded
by 2 or more notches in the last 12 months.

## 9. Pricing and collateral

* Pricing: base rate (external benchmark linked) plus a spread by Internal Risk Grade:
  grade 1 = 1.00 percent, grade 2 = 1.40 percent, grade 3 = 1.90 percent, grade 4 = 2.50
  percent, grade 5 = 3.25 percent, grade 6 = 4.25 percent. Grades 7 and 8 are not priced.
* Collateral: primary security is hypothecation of current assets for working capital and
  first charge on financed fixed assets for term loans. Collateral security of at least 25
  percent of the sanctioned limit is required for grades 4 to 6. Grades 1 to 3 may be
  sanctioned on primary security alone.
* Covenants: borrower must maintain net debt / EBITDA of at most 4.5 times, DSCR of at least
  1.25 times, and not pay dividends if either is breached; promoter pledge must stay at or
  below 50 percent of promoter holding; quarterly stock and debtor statements are mandatory.

## 10. Bank-defined scores

The bank reports three scores in every credit review. Each is defined here and formalised
in the file `policy.lean`.

### 10.1 Financial Strength Score (FSS), 0 to 100

The FSS is the sum of six band scores. Each band uses the latest audited year. Amounts are
INR crore (the Lean formalisation uses crore multiplied by 100).

| Component (maximum points) | Band, points awarded |
|---|---|
| Net debt / EBITDA (25) | at most 1.5 times: 25; above 1.5 and at most 2.5: 20; above 2.5 and at most 3.5: 14; above 3.5 and at most 4.5: 7; above 4.5 or EBITDA not positive: 0 |
| Interest coverage, EBITDA / interest (20) | at least 6.0 times: 20; at least 4.0: 15; at least 2.5: 10; at least 1.5: 5; below 1.5: 0 |
| DSCR, CFADS / debt service (20) | at least 2.00 times: 20; at least 1.50: 15; at least 1.25: 10; at least 1.00: 4; below 1.00: 0 |
| Current ratio (15) | at least 1.50 times: 15; at least 1.25: 11; at least 1.00: 6; below 1.00: 0 |
| Total debt / equity (10) | at most 1.0 times: 10; above 1.0 and at most 2.0: 7; above 2.0 and at most 3.0: 3; above 3.0 or equity not positive: 0 |
| Revenue growth year on year (10) | at least +10 percent: 10; at least 0 percent: 6; at least -10 percent: 2; below -10 percent: 0 |

Zero or negative interest expense is treated as meeting every interest coverage band. FSS
bands: **Strong** 80 to 100; **Adequate** 60 to 79; **Moderate** 40 to 59; **Weak** 0 to 39.

### 10.2 Internal Risk Grade (IRG), 1 to 8

Grade 1 is the best credit quality and grade 8 is decline. It is computed in four steps.

1. Base grade from the FSS: 85 or more: 1; 75 to 84: 2; 65 to 74: 3; 55 to 64: 4; 45 to 54:
   5; 35 to 44: 6; 25 to 34: 7; below 25: 8.
2. Rating floor from the external rating index (section 5): index 1 to 3: 1; 4 to 6: 2;
   7 to 8: 3; 9: 4; 10: 5; 11 to 13: 6; 14 to 16: 7; 17 and above: 8. The rating floor is
   the best grade the borrower can obtain; the grade after this step is the larger
   (worse) of the base grade and the rating floor.
3. Red-flag notches: add the number of major red flags (section 6), at most 3 notches.
4. Cap at 8. If the default flag is raised the grade is 8 regardless of the above.

Interpretation of the final grade: 1 to 3 = low risk, 4 to 5 = acceptable risk, 6 to 7 =
high risk (credit committee only), 8 = decline.

### 10.3 Maximum Exposure

The maximum exposure for a borrower is set by two caps, each scaled by an exposure
percentage P determined by the Internal Risk Grade: grade 1 or 2: 100; grade 3 or 4: 75;
grade 5: 50; grade 6: 25; grade 7 or 8: 0.

* Turnover cap: P percent of 20 percent of projected annual turnover (section 7).
* Net-worth cap: P percent of tangible net worth.

The maximum exposure is the smaller of the two caps. A proposed aggregate bank exposure is
**within limit** if it is no more than both caps, written without division as
500 x proposed <= P x projected turnover and 100 x proposed <= P x tangible net worth;
otherwise it is **exceeds limit**. Aggregate exposure includes existing fund-based and
non-fund-based limits with the bank.

## 11. Decision rules

* Approve: FSS in Strong or Adequate, IRG 1 to 3, no major red flag, exposure within limit.
* Approve with conditions: IRG 4 to 5, or one major red flag, or exposure reduced to the
  maximum exposure; conditions as per section 9.
* Refer: IRG 6 to 7, two or more major red flags, or any caution-list item.
* Decline: IRG 8, default flag, negative-list item, or ineligible under section 2.
* A proposal that fails any minimum standard in section 4 is not approved at the requested
  amount: the recommendation is to reduce the credit line or decline, naming each failed
  criterion.
